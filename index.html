Chapter 011947–1964

The Foundation & Nation-Building Era

How do you turn independence into a republic?

Freedom brought both possibility and catastrophe. India had to accommodate displaced families, integrate princely states and create institutions for a vast democracy. Under Jawaharlal Nehru, public investment and planning became central to economic policy.

Keep in view

Political equality arrived before economic equality. A common citizenship had to coexist with deep differences of language, caste, religion and income.

1947DemocracyIndependence, and the trauma of Partition

British rule ended on 15 August. Partition created India and Pakistan and triggered mass displacement and communal violence.

Context & significance

Freedom and forced migration belonged to the same moment. Rehabilitation and the protection of minorities became urgent tasks for the new state.

1947–1950DemocracyBringing the princely states into the Union

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and V. P. Menon helped bring more than 500 princely states into India through accession and integration.

Context & significance

The process was not uniform. Junagadh, Hyderabad and Jammu and Kashmir followed different, contested paths. Territorial integration also continued beyond 1950.

1949–1950DemocracyA Constitution, then a republic

The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. Most provisions came into force on 26 January 1950, when India became a republic.

Context & significance

The Constitution established parliamentary government, fundamental rights and a federal structure. Its original Preamble described a Sovereign Democratic Republic. The words Socialist and Secular were added in 1976.

1951EconomyPlanning begins with farms and irrigation

The First Five-Year Plan prioritised agriculture, irrigation and recovery after Partition. Planning aimed to direct scarce capital towards development.

Context & significance

The Second Plan, beginning in 1956, put greater emphasis on heavy industry and the public sector. Agriculture and industry were successive priorities, not a single policy launched in 1951.

1951–1952DemocracyThe first general election

India held its first general election under universal adult franchise in 1951–1952. Congress won, and Nehru continued as prime minister.

Context & significance

A competitive election made constitutional democracy a working practice. The initial voting age was 21. It was lowered to 18 through the 61st Amendment in 1988.

1956DemocracyLanguage reshapes the map

The States Reorganisation Act reorganised state boundaries substantially along linguistic lines. Demands for language-based states had already produced Andhra State in 1953.

Context & significance

Reorganisation accommodated regional identities within the Union. It did not settle every boundary dispute, and further states were created in later decades.

The Sino-Indian War exposed serious weaknesses in India's defence preparedness and strained the assumptions behind relations with China.

Context & significance

The conflict pushed defence and border security higher on the national agenda. Non-alignment did not remove the need for military capability.

Chapter 021964–1975

Wars, Food Security & Social Transition

What does security mean when food is scarce?

Leadership changed after Nehru's death in 1964. War, drought and food shortages put immediate pressure on the state. Agricultural technology, public procurement and bank nationalisation changed how government sought to deliver security and development.

Keep in view

Higher output did not mean equal gains. Access to irrigation, credit and markets shaped who benefited from agricultural change.

1965ConflictWar and the call for food security

India and Pakistan fought their second war in 1965. Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri's Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan linked the soldier and the farmer.

Context & significance

Military security and food supply became closely connected national priorities. The Tashkent agreement followed in January 1966.

Mid-1960s onwardSocietyThe Green Revolution changes farming

High-yielding seeds, irrigation, fertiliser and procurement support raised grain output, especially wheat. M. S. Swaminathan was a key scientific leader among many contributors.

Context & significance

Gains were uneven across crops, regions and farm sizes. Food security improved over time, rather than arriving immediately with the introduction of new seeds.

1967DemocracyCongress loses ground in the states

The 1967 elections weakened Congress dominance and brought non-Congress governments to several states.

Context & significance

Opposition coordination and regional politics grew in importance well before the coalition governments of the 1990s.

1969EconomyFourteen banks are nationalised

Indira Gandhi's government nationalised 14 major commercial banks. Six more were nationalised in 1980.

Context & significance

The state used banking to pursue wider access to credit and rural branches. Nationalisation was an institutional change, not proof that every lending objective was achieved.

NDDB launched Operation Flood in 1970. Under Verghese Kurien's leadership, dairy cooperatives connected producers with urban markets.

Context & significance

The programme ran in phases through 1996. The White Revolution was a distinct dairy transformation, not part of the Green Revolution's grain technology package.

The liberation struggle in East Pakistan, repression and a major refugee influx preceded the December India–Pakistan war. Pakistan's forces in the east surrendered on 16 December.

Bangladesh emerged as an independent country. Its liberation involved Bangladeshi resistance and Indian military intervention, rather than a war fought by India alone.

Chapter 031975–1991

Political Turmoil & the Coalition Turn

How resilient are democratic institutions?

The Emergency placed extraordinary power in the executive. Its end brought an electoral transfer of power, but the following years also saw political violence, regional conflict and new demands for representation. Telecom research advanced as national politics became more fragmented.

Keep in view

This chapter extends the original 1975–1989 period through the 1990 transition, so the story reaches the 1991 reforms without a missing interval.

1975–1977DemocracyThe Emergency curtails civil liberties

A national Emergency was proclaimed on 25 June 1975 under Indira Gandhi. Opposition leaders were detained and the press faced censorship.

Context & significance

Coercive sterilisation and demolitions became enduring symbols of abuse. This internal Emergency must be distinguished from earlier emergencies declared over external threats.

1977DemocracyVoters remove the Congress government

The Janata Party won the general election, and Morarji Desai led the first non-Congress government at the Union level.

Context & significance

The result demonstrated the force of electoral accountability. Internal divisions later weakened Janata, and Congress returned to power in 1980.

1978DemocracyConstitutional safeguards after the Emergency

The 44th Amendment introduced safeguards against another internal Emergency, including replacing internal disturbance with armed rebellion as a constitutional ground.

Context & significance

It also protected enforcement of the rights under Articles 20 and 21 from suspension through Article 359. Legal safeguards were one response to the experience of 1975–1977.

The victory became a lasting sporting landmark. It should not be treated as the sole cause of cricket's later commercial and broadcasting expansion.

Chapter 041991–2004

Economic Liberalisation & Globalisation

What changes when an economy opens up?

A balance-of-payments crisis forced urgent decisions in 1991. Reforms reduced industrial licensing and opened more space for trade, private investment and competition. Coalition governments continued the broad shift while security crises and communal conflict challenged the country.

Keep in view

Reform was a process across several governments. The expression LPG is useful shorthand, but it hides differences between liberalisation, privatisation and global integration.

1991EconomyA crisis accelerates economic reform

Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh led reforms during a severe foreign-exchange crisis. Industrial delicensing, trade changes and financial-sector reforms followed.

Context & significance

The shift had roots in earlier policy changes. It did not abolish all licences, remove the public sector or produce equal outcomes across regions and workers.

The Babri Masjid was demolished on 6 December 1992. Communal violence followed.

The 73rd and 74th Amendments were passed in 1992 and came into force in 1993, strengthening the constitutional framework for panchayats and municipalities.

Context & significance

They did not invent local government. They gave it a stronger constitutional basis, with elections and reservation provisions. Actual devolution still depended on state action.

Chapter 052004–2014

Economic Expansion & Rights-Based Governance

How does growth become an entitlement?

The UPA governments led by Manmohan Singh combined economic expansion with legislation on information, employment and welfare. Space missions and nuclear diplomacy expanded international engagement. Terror attacks, the global financial crisis and corruption controversies tested institutions.

Keep in view

Passing a law and delivering its promise are different things. Implementation, public accountability and access shaped the reach of new entitlements.

2005DemocracyThe right to ask the state for information

The Right to Information Act created a national framework for access to information held by public authorities, subject to specified exemptions.

Context & significance

It gave citizens a route to seek records and challenge refusals. Transparency depended on functioning information commissions, record keeping and enforcement.

Read the record
  • India Code · Right to Information Act, 2005 ↗
Link to this event ↗
2005–2006SocietyRural employment becomes a legal guarantee

The employment guarantee law was enacted in 2005 and began rolling out in 2006. It guarantees at least 100 days of unskilled manual work per financial year to a rural household whose adult members volunteer for it.

Context & significance

The entitlement is per household, not per individual. The original NREGA name was changed to MGNREGA in 2009.

2008EconomyThe civil nuclear agreement

India and the United States signed their civil nuclear cooperation agreement on 10 October 2008, following years of negotiation and an NSG waiver.

Context & significance

It widened the scope for international civilian nuclear cooperation. It did not confer NPT nuclear-weapon State status or resolve every commercial and liability issue.

The attacks prompted security changes, including the establishment of the National Investigation Agency and attention to coastal security. These changes require separate evaluation of their implementation.

Aadhaar later became part of welfare and digital service delivery. Identity infrastructure also raised questions about privacy, authentication failures and exclusion. It is not proof of citizenship.

Civil society campaigns, including Anna Hazare's movement, pressed for stronger anti-corruption institutions and a Lokpal law.

The protests became a major political force. Parliament passed the Lokpal and Lokayuktas legislation in 2013. It received presidential assent in January 2014, with implementation unfolding later.

Chapter 062014–present

Digital Transformation & Modern Geopolitics

What does transformation look like in daily life?

Digital payments, tax reform and infrastructure have changed how citizens and businesses interact with the state and the economy. These years also brought the pandemic, constitutional changes in Jammu and Kashmir, and prominent space and diplomatic milestones.

Keep in view

The present is unfinished history. A programme's launch is evidence of policy intent. Its effect on jobs, public welfare or the environment needs separate evidence over time. Selected coverage here ends in July 2025.

2014EconomyMake in India begins

Make in India launched on 25 September 2014 to encourage investment and manufacturing.

Context & significance

It belongs at the start of this period, rather than being described as a new 2024 initiative. Programme goals and measured manufacturing outcomes are different claims.

April 2016ScienceUPI connects bank accounts in real time

NPCI's Unified Payments Interface had its pilot launch on 11 April 2016, before demonetisation. It enabled instant payments across participating banks.

Context & significance

UPI built on an existing banking and payments system. Its later adoption should not be reduced to a single policy or a single political term.

What this edition covers

Selected milestones from 1947 to July 2025. This is a curated history, not a complete record of events through 2026.

The six eras are editorial groupings, not official historical categories. Boundary years overlap when a transition crosses governments or institutions.

How to read the stories

Each event separates a short factual account from context and significance. The latter includes editorial interpretation. Source links sit inside each expanded story.

Institutional records help establish dates, decisions and programme design. They do not, by themselves, establish every claimed policy outcome.

What to keep questioning

National milestones do not capture every regional, social or personal experience. This is a starting point for further reading, not a ranking of prime ministers or a verdict on any party.
